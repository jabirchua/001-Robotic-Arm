## 1. Purpose

This document defines the preliminary component mass estimates and gravitational loading assumptions for Project 001.

The objectives are to:

- Establish an initial mass budget for the robotic arm.
- Estimate static torque requirements at the shoulder (J2) and elbow (J3).
- Identify unknown component masses and geometric properties.
- Provide a foundation for motor, gearbox, bearing, and structural selection.
- Maintain traceability as estimated masses are replaced by CAD-derived or measured values.

All torque calculations are provisional and must be revised as the mechanical design develops.

### 3.1 Interpretation

These masses are preliminary design inputs rather than verified physical measurements.

The upper-arm and forearm masses represent their respective structural assemblies, subject to confirmation of which fasteners, bearings, and actuators are included.

The elbow mechanism is treated as a separate assembly from the upper arm and forearm.

The complete wrist mechanism is treated as one lumped assembly until its internal component masses and centres of mass become available.

The tool flange and gripper are treated as one assembly, separate from the payload.

The payload mass of 500 g represents the external object being carried and excludes the gripper and flange.

### 3.2 Unknown Masses

The following masses have not yet been established:

- Base structure and mounting hardware.
- Shoulder actuator and transmission, unless already included in another mass estimate.
- Base rotation mechanism.
- Electrical wiring and cable-management components.
- Additional bearings, fasteners, and structural reinforcement not included in the listed assemblies.

These unknown masses must not be assigned arbitrary numerical values.

The current 1.700 kg subtotal is therefore not the total robot mass.

## 4. Static Load Analysis

### 4.1 Fundamental Equation

The gravitational force generated by a component is:

$$
F_g=mg
$$

Where:

- $
- F_g
- $ = gravitational force (N)
- $m$ = component mass (kg)
- $g$ = gravitational acceleration (9.81 m/s²)

The static torque about a joint is:

$$
\tau=F_gd=mgd
$$

Where \(d\) is the perpendicular distance between the joint axis and the component's gravitational force line of action.

For multiple components:

$$
\tau_{\text{joint}}=\sum_i m_i g d_i
$$

The distances must be measured relative to the joint being analysed.

## 5. Shoulder Joint (J2) Load Analysis

### 5.1 Components Contributing to Shoulder Torque

The shoulder must resist gravitational torque from the components supported downstream of the shoulder joint.

For the preliminary model, these include:

- Upper arm
- Elbow mechanism
- Forearm
- Wrist mechanism
- Tool flange and gripper
- Payload

Any additional downstream components not included in these estimates must be incorporated later.

### 5.2 General Static Torque Equation

$$
\tau_{J2}=g(
m_u d_u+
m_e d_e+
m_f d_f+
m_w d_w+
m_t d_t+
m_p d_p)
$$

Where the $d$ terms represent the horizontal moment arms of each component's centre of mass relative to the shoulder axis.

### 5.3 Illustrative Horizontal-Arm Configuration

For a provisional calculation only, assume:

- Upper arm and forearm are straight and horizontal.
- Upper-arm and forearm masses are uniformly distributed.
- The elbow mechanism centre of mass is located at the elbow axis.
- The wrist mechanism centre of mass is located at the wrist joint, 420 mm from the shoulder.
- The tool flange, gripper, and payload are represented at the nominal 495 mm endpoint.

The assumed moment arms are:

| Component | Moment arm (m) |
|---|---:|
| Upper arm | 0.110 |
| Elbow mechanism | 0.220 |
| Forearm | 0.320 |
| Wrist mechanism | 0.420 |
| Tool flange and gripper | 0.495 |
| Payload | 0.495 |

Therefore:

$$
\tau_{J2}=9.81[
(0.350)(0.110)+
(0.200)(0.220)+
(0.300)(0.320)+
(0.250)(0.420)+
(0.100)(0.495)+
(0.500)(0.495)]
$$

$$
\boxed{\tau_{J2}\approx5.695\ \text{N·m}}
$$

This is the estimated static gravitational holding torque for the stated illustrative configuration and included masses only.

It is not a verified maximum shoulder torque or a final motor requirement.

## 6. Elbow Joint (J3) Load Analysis

### 6.1 Components Contributing to Elbow Torque

For the preliminary model, the elbow must support the downstream components:

- Forearm
- Wrist mechanism
- Tool flange and gripper
- Payload

The upper arm is not included because its weight acts on the shoulder-side structure.

The elbow mechanism's own weight is not included in the elbow torque calculation because its centre of mass is provisionally assumed to lie on the elbow axis.

This assumption must be reviewed once the elbow mechanism's actual geometry is known.

### 6.2 General Static Torque Equation

$$
\tau_{J3}=g(
m_f d_f+
m_w d_w+
m_t d_t+
m_p d_p)
$$

### 6.3 Illustrative Horizontal-Forearm Configuration

Assume:

| Component | Moment arm (m) |
|---|---:|
| Forearm | 0.100 |
| Wrist mechanism | 0.200 |
| Tool flange and gripper | 0.275 |
| Payload | 0.275 |

Then:

$$
\tau_{J3}=9.81[
(0.300)(0.100)+
(0.250)(0.200)+
(0.100)(0.275)+
(0.500)(0.275)]
$$

$$
\boxed{\tau_{J3}\approx2.403\ \text{N·m}}
$$

This is the estimated static gravitational holding torque for the illustrative elbow configuration and included masses only.

## 7. Preliminary Torque Summary

| Joint | Illustrative static torque |
|---|---:|
| Shoulder (J2) | 5.695 N·m |
| Elbow (J3) | 2.403 N·m |

**Important:** These results are not motor selection ratings.

Actual joint torque requirements may change due to:

- Joint orientation and arm configuration.
- Actual centres of mass.
- Missing downstream component masses.
- Acceleration and deceleration.
- Gearbox efficiency and friction.
- Transmission backlash and mechanical losses.
- External forces during manipulation.
- Required design safety margins.

A motor and transmission must also be evaluated for continuous holding capability, peak torque, speed, thermal limits, and any braking or counterbalancing arrangement.

## 8. Assumptions and Limitations

1. Gravitational acceleration is taken as 9.81 m/s².
2. All component masses are preliminary estimates.
3. The illustrative torque calculations assume a straight horizontal arm.
4. Upper-arm and forearm centres of mass are assumed to be at their geometric midpoints.
5. The elbow mechanism centre of mass is assumed to coincide with the elbow axis.
6. The wrist mechanism is represented by a lumped mass at the wrist joint.
7. The tool flange, gripper, and payload are represented at the nominal endpoint.
8. Unknown masses are excluded rather than estimated.
9. Dynamic loading, transmission losses, and safety factors are not included.
10. The results are provisional and do not demonstrate that the arm can safely hold the target payload.

The assumed mass locations should be replaced with CAD-derived centres of mass as the design matures.
